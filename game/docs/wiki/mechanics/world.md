# World

The map, sites, guard guns, time of day, weather and engine heat. The principles behind them are in [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md).

## Map and sites

The map is a grid of tiles with a height on every tile corner, so the ground is smooth hills and valleys. Rain cut gullies and dry washes into it, slopes slid into scree, dry lakes left salt crust and mud, and wind piled deep sand into dune ridges up to about 3 m tall. Boulders lie at cliff feet and on ridges. Ruins of the old world stand on flat ground near today's places, on hill edges and on road bends: houses, silos, water towers, gas stations, dead fields, power lines, billboards and tank hulks. Faded asphalt roads join the old settlements. A few old highways ended in broken bridges over deep gorges. At Broken Wing a crashed ship's wing lies on lattice and pylons beside a crash trench. The road runs under the wing's bent root, a tall hoop, then up a sand ramp and along the wing's top, and trucks drive down at the far end. Ruins and structures block driving and sight, so they give cover. Squatters live in shack camps outside towns, among ruins and at junctions, behind wooden fences and junk piles. Burnt cars line the roads. Scrub grows where water gathers, and small basins hold dirty water or toxic pools that slow trucks. Everything collides by its real shape: trucks see over low fences, junk and wrecks, but not over walls. Each tile has a terrain type: road, hardpan, loose sand, scrub, scree, mud, gravel, salt crust, cracked asphalt, ash, dirt track, irrigation canal or cracked concrete. Each type has its own driving speed. Uphill slows a truck, downhill speeds it up a little. Tiles too steep to climb are cliffs, and routes go around them. Hills and obstacles block sight, and the fog of war shows only what the truck sees. A trail of crashed-ship debris (hull plates, wing shards, escape pods, habitat cylinders and power cells) runs from Podfield past Broken Wing to the Fallen Sun's north-east rim, with lone pods and plates across the basin. It is cover and scenery only: it blocks driving and sight like other props, and holds no loot.

Danger is set by region, not by player level.

Faction squads roam the map. Places are discovered by exploring. Towns and locations are static places that trucks never enter, so they block driving. Small locations have one gate, where the first road crosses their edge. Towns and large locations have a gate on every road. Each gate post carries a lamp. A dust pad lies outside each gate. A site is used only from a pad. Towns have a wall, and other sites have palisades, stone walls, wreck walls or fences. The truck must be stopped to use a town or search salvage. In reach but still moving, the action shows dimmed.

The Fallen Sun is a territory: a crater with no edge, gates or pads, so trucks drive in. It is a place to prowl, hide and fight over loot on the ruins of a ship.

- **The wreck.** One broken colony ship lies across the crater, laid out from the committee's level concept. An open ribcage tube runs north from the south floor to a squat hub with a ring on top at the centre. A broken spine leads from the hub to the bow, the largest piece, which lies with its closed nose against the north rim. Around them lie two half-buried arch shells, three ring-banded drums, shard clusters, a tilted tower slab, a fallen gantry and a few huts.
- **Inside the hull.** The ribcage tube and the arch shells are open at both ends and high overhead, so a truck drives in and through. Their walls block driving and sight, so a truck inside is hidden from the sides and seen only along the tube. No hull piece is a deck a truck climbs onto.
- **Basin.** The crater is an irregular basin, not a circle. Grey crag walls close the north and north-east as real cliffs that block driving and sight, with one drivable gap between them where a dirt road climbs out north. A red-brown scree slope runs down the west bank, a broad hill rises on the east rim, and the south lies open. Low swells roll across the floor. The floor has the wasteland's own sand, so nothing marks where the territory starts.
- **Dirt roads.** A web of grey-brown dirt roads, 10 to 12 m wide, loops round every large piece, so each stands on its own island of pale sand with two or more ways round it. An outer ring joins the three road ends, and spurs fray out past the edge into the wasteland and lead nowhere. Dirt roads are track ground: faster than loose sand, so routes follow them.
- **Debris field.** Plates, junk and dead trucks lie scattered on the islands, thicker in the south and sparse to the north, and pale plate fragments lie on the scree. Debris never lands on a dirt road. It blocks driving and sight like any prop, and gives cover and ambush lines.
- **Crash furrow and wing.** Past the open south rim a furrow runs about 180 m south-south-west, where the ship came in. A huge torn wing lies along it, 32 m wide. A truck drives up a ramp onto it from the ground, about 6 m up, crosses 88 m of span over two large hull drums that stick out on both sides, and comes down the far end. The wing's sides and anything under it are out of reach.
- **Flaps.** Four wing flaps stand propped up as jump ramps, two in the furrow and two on dirt road stretches of the crater floor. Each is 20 m long and about 1.4 m high at its lip, with clear ground ahead. A truck that drives off a lip at road speed flies and lands upright. The landing costs its wheels some wear, less than a breakdown. Routes never take a lip, so a jump is a move the player makes in manual driving.
- **Loot spots.** There are 24, each near a dirt road. Nine are caches inside or beside the hull pieces, two of them in the ribcage tube and one in each shell, and hold the old site loot. Sight inside the hull is short and an ambush can wait at an open end. The other 15 are field spots in the debris, four of them in the furrow beside the wing and the flaps, with a scrap-heavy road-wreck load. A truck parks beside a spot and searches it like a road wreck. A spot never leaves, and an emptied one refills over about two weeks, so some part of the territory always holds something.
- **Roads.** Three dead-end roads come down the basin bank from the west, the east and the south-east and end just inside its edge, where they meet the dirt road ring. The south-east road leaves the end of the Kiln Camp track, so raiders have a short way in. No road crosses the crater, so traffic does not cut through it.
- **NPCs.** Scavengers search the spots, inside the hull too. Raiders and vultures wait at the road ends and in the debris fields.
- **Reactor.** The ship's core glows green-teal in a breach in the bow's flank. A truck within about 32 m of it costs its driver health each turn, down to a floor, so it never kills by itself. The player may drive in by hand. NPC routes keep out of the zone.

Old Orchard is a territory too: a ruined farm under military occupation, in a wide basin west of the north trunk road. Like the Fallen Sun it has no edge, gates or pads, so trucks drive in. It has no reactor or hazard. Its edge is not a circle: it follows the basin's ridges and roads, about 430 m from south to north and 330 m across, and takes in a north-west pocket and the flat ground north-east of the north ridge.

- **Roads and entries.** Five roads cross the farm and leave it in five directions. A straight old asphalt road runs up the basin a little east of north and leaves by the north edge. A short spur road from the north trunk road ends at its south end. Four broad dirt roads leave it too: the crossroad curves from the farmhouse yard over the highway, round the blockhouse and out east to the trunk road; the south-west road runs past the motor pool and the barn and out south; the depot road runs past the hangars and out east to the trunk road; and the north field road runs west through the north-west pocket. Narrow tracks join the groves and the depot. Trucks also drive in over open ground. Concrete barriers stand askew on the road shoulders at the crossroads and the south entry.
- **Buildings.** They stand at their real size against an 8 m army truck. A ruined two-storey farmhouse, 26 m long, stands west of the road at the middle, and a sandbagged concrete blockhouse just east of it commands the crossroads. Three rusty Quonset hangars, each about 22 m long, stand side by side on packed dirt to the north-east, and a fourth stands at the north-east depot. To the south-west, a gabled barn and its shed stand past a concrete motor pool where six army trucks are parked at uneven angles. A second barn stands in the north-west pocket. Guard huts with sandbag corners stand where roads enter. A derelict jeep and an army truck stand on the highway's shoulders, one truck broke down on the depot road and one ran off the south-west road.
- **Loot spots.** There are 28, and each is its own stock. The farmhouse holds the rich landmark load. The barns hold grain, cloth, a little scrap and fuel. The hangars, the blockhouse, the guard huts and four army crate stacks hold army stores: parts, meds and scrap, with the best chance at a weapon or armor. The army trucks hold a road-wreck load. A truck parks beside a spot and searches it, and an emptied spot refills over about two weeks like the Fallen Sun's.
- **Groves.** About 500 dead trees stand in 15 blocks on both sides of the road and in the north grounds, on darker field ground, each block in rows along or across the road and set a little off its line. Single trees stand between the blocks, so the groves fray at their edges. Rows are 12 m apart and trees 6 m apart in a row, with a few missing. Each tree's trunk is narrow and its wide crown starts above a truck's roof, so a truck drives the lanes between rows. Rows block sight across them but not along the lanes, which gives ambush cover. Broken wooden fences edge the outer blocks.
- **Breakable trees.** A tree holds like a wall until a truck hits it at 3 m/s or more, which breaks it, scrapes the truck and slows it. That opens the row and the line of sight. Broken trees grow back after 3 days, only out of the player's gray vision. Gunfire breaks nothing.
- **Canals and dirt roads.** Blue-grey concrete irrigation canals run along both sides of the highway, broken where roads cross, and along the block edges. A truck in one drags and wears like dirty water. Dirt roads and tracks are packed pale dirt, as fast as hardpan.
- **Clutter and debris.** Fuel drums lie by the trucks and hangars, lumber and junk by the barns and the farmhouse, and loose sandbags and barriers by the guard huts, all scattered, never in rows. Junk piles and old car wrecks lie along the road.
- **NPCs.** Scavengers search the spots, and raiders and vultures hunt there.

Each town gate has a guard gun. Each turn it shoots the nearest vehicle in its range that fired at anyone but a raider, whatever its own faction. Raiders also trade in towns, so guards judge by action. A town gate is therefore a safe place to run to.

Raiders have two camps, Scrapjaw Camp in the north and Kiln Camp in the south. A dirt track leads to each camp gate. Raiders spawn outside a camp gate and patrol the roads around their own camp between raids. Each camp gate gun shoots the nearest non-raider in its range every turn, whether or not that vehicle fired. The player cannot use camp services.

## Sun, time and weather

A day is a fixed number of turns, and the clock starts in the morning. The sun rises in the east, crosses north at noon and sets in the west. There is no sun at night. A point is in shade when a hill, rock, wreck or building blocks the ray toward the sun. Heat is at its base rate in shade and at night, and rises toward its peak in full sun. Far from the player, shade is not computed and every point counts as open sun. Heat multiplies fuel and supply drain, so the wrong time and route cost more fuel and supplies. The ground darkens over explored shaded tiles, by the same rule the sim uses. The sun's light follows the clock, and the scene dims after dark.

Weather events come from the world's own randomness, so a seed replays the same weather. Dust storms are huge moving areas, and several can roam the region at once. Inside one, sight and aim both suffer, top speed drops, and wear climbs faster. The effects fade in over the outer 25 tiles of a storm, so riding in is gradual. A heat wave covers the whole region and strengthens the sun's heat. Overcast also covers the region, and cancels the sun's heat. The HUD shows the day, time, current heat and the region's weather.

The sun also heats the player's running engine, faster at higher speed. Airflow, shade, night and parking cool it, and parking in shade cools it fastest. Full noon sun at top speed overheats a cold stock engine in about 25 turns, while morning and evening sun barely warm it. An overheated engine loses HP every turn it keeps driving. Every engine starts heating at the same sun heat, where the ground shimmers, and engines differ only in how fast they heat. A racing V6 or a turbine overheats fast in the noon sun, and a diesel runs cool. Still, every engine overheats on the shortest Bowl to Nose trip at top speed, leaving at 10:00. The HUD shows the engine temperature as a gauge, and the log warns once when it runs hot. NPCs have no engine heat, since they have no rule for stopping to cool down.

Engine overdrive, on the O key, gives the player 33% more top speed and acceleration. It heats a driving engine in any sun. A cold engine overheats in about 16 turns at night and 8 in full noon sun. The G key pours 1 supply of water over the engine and takes away half of the full heat at once, with a cloud of steam.

The L key switches the player's headlights on and off at any time, even while a turn plays. NPC lamps come on at dusk and go off at dawn. The lamps light the way at night and change no sight or detection rule.
