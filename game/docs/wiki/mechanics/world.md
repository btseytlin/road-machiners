# World

The map, sites, guard guns, time of day, weather and engine heat. The principles behind them are in [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md).

## Map and sites

The map is a grid of tiles with a height on every tile corner, so the ground is smooth hills and valleys. Rain cut gullies and dry washes into it, slopes slid into scree, dry lakes left salt crust and mud, and wind piled deep sand into dune ridges up to about 3 m tall. Boulders lie at cliff feet and on ridges. Ruins of the old world stand on flat ground near today's places, on hill edges and on road bends: houses, silos, water towers, gas stations, dead fields, power lines, billboards and tank hulks. Faded asphalt roads join the old settlements. A few old highways ended in broken bridges over deep gorges. At Broken Wing a crashed ship's wing lies on lattice and pylons beside a crash trench. The road runs under the wing's bent root, a tall hoop, then up a sand ramp and along the wing's top, and trucks drive down at the far end. Ruins and structures block driving and sight, so they give cover. Squatters live in shack camps outside towns, among ruins and at junctions, behind wooden fences and junk piles. Burnt cars line the roads. Scrub grows where water gathers, and small basins hold dirty water or toxic pools that slow trucks. Everything collides by its real shape: trucks see over low fences, junk and wrecks, but not over walls. Each tile has a terrain type: road, hardpan, loose sand, scrub, scree, mud, gravel, salt crust, cracked asphalt, ash or hull plating. Each type has its own driving speed. Uphill slows a truck, downhill speeds it up a little. Tiles too steep to climb are cliffs, and routes go around them. Hills and obstacles block sight, and the fog of war shows only what the truck sees.

Danger is set by region, not by player level.

Faction squads roam the map. Places are discovered by exploring. Towns and locations are static places that trucks never enter, so they block driving. Small locations have one gate, where the first road crosses their edge. Towns and large locations have a gate on every road. Each gate carries a lamp on each side. The gatehouses of towns and raider camps carry the gate gun. A dust pad lies outside each gate. A site is used only from a pad. The ten inhabited sites (both towns, the oases, the pump station, the granary, the salvage yard, the lock and the raider camps) are fortresses: a circular, square or star-shaped curtain of walls at least three times as tall as a truck, with towers and a shut gatehouse at each gate. Their walls block driving and sight by their real shape, and trucks stay outside and use the pads. The abandoned sites keep wreck walls or fences. A fortress is discovered by seeing the ground near its pads. The truck must be stopped to use a town or search salvage. In reach but still moving, the action shows dimmed.

The Fallen Sun is a territory: a crater with no edge, gates or pads, so trucks drive in. It is a place to prowl, hide and fight over loot on the ruins of a ship.

- **The wreck.** The colony ship broke its back along one crash line from the north-west rim to the south-east rim. The bow lies nose-up with its torn end high over the floor. The forward hull is long and nearly flat. The engine core is split open around the reactor. The aft hull tilts up toward the south-east bank. The stern is a wall of plating rolled onto its side, which is cover, not a deck. Two hull plates thrown off the line to the north-east and south-west are small ramps that make sniper perches.
- **Hull decks.** The bow, forward hull, aft hull and both plates are decks a truck drives onto. A deck is ground, so it blocks sight and casts shade like a hill. Its plating is smooth and fast and raises little dust. A truck gets on by the low end, where the deck meets the floor. The sides and the high end drop to the floor. Where the drop is steep, it is a cliff no truck climbs, and a truck that drives off it falls. The torn bow end stands up to about 8 m high. Ribs arch across the bigger decks. Only their legs block, so trucks drive through under the arches.
- **Debris field.** Plates, ribs, thruster housings and dead trucks lie along the crash line and thin out toward the rim. They block driving and sight like any prop, and give cover and ambush lines.
- **Loot spots.** There are 24. Nine are deck bays: crates on the decks that hold the old site loot. They stand on high ground, so a truck searching one is seen from far off. The other 15 are hull caches in the debris field, with a scrap-heavy road-wreck load. A truck parks beside a spot and searches it like a road wreck. A spot never leaves, and an emptied one refills over about two weeks, so some part of the territory always holds something.
- **Roads.** Three dead-end roads come down the crater bank from the west, the east and the south, and end on the floor in gaps between the hull sections. The south road branches off the Kiln Camp track, so raiders have a short way in. No road crosses the crater, so traffic does not cut through it.
- **NPCs.** Scavengers search the spots. Raiders and vultures wait at the road ends and along both sides of the crash line.
- **Reactor.** A glowing reactor core stands in a scorched pit at the centre. A truck within about 32 m of it costs its driver health each turn, down to a floor, so it never kills by itself. The player may drive in by hand. NPC routes keep out of the zone.

Old Orchard is a territory too: a ruined farm under military occupation, in a wide basin west of the north trunk road. Like the Fallen Sun it has no edge, gates or pads, so trucks drive in. It has no reactor or hazard. Its edge is not a circle: it follows the basin's ridges and roads, about 430 m from south to north and 330 m across, and takes in a north-west pocket and the flat ground north-east of the north ridge.

- **Roads and entries.** Five roads cross the farm and leave it in five directions. A straight old asphalt road runs up the basin a little east of north and leaves by the north edge. A short spur road from the north trunk road ends at its south end. Four broad dirt roads leave it too: the crossroad curves from the farmhouse yard over the highway, round the blockhouse and out east to the trunk road; the south-west road runs past the motor pool and the barn and out south; the depot road runs past the hangars and out east to the trunk road; and the north field road runs west through the north-west pocket. Narrow tracks join the groves and the depot. Trucks also drive in over open ground. Concrete barriers stand askew on the road shoulders at the crossroads and the south entry.
- **Buildings.** They stand at their real size against an 8 m army truck. A ruined two-storey farmhouse, 26 m long, stands west of the road at the middle, and a sandbagged concrete blockhouse just east of it commands the crossroads. Three rusty Quonset hangars, each about 22 m long, stand side by side on packed dirt to the north-east, and a fourth stands at the north-east depot. To the south-west, a gabled barn and its shed stand past a concrete motor pool where six army trucks are parked at uneven angles. A second barn stands in the north-west pocket. Guard huts with sandbag corners stand where roads enter. A derelict jeep and an army truck stand on the highway's shoulders, one truck broke down on the depot road and one ran off the south-west road.
- **Loot spots.** There are 28, and each is its own stock. The farmhouse holds the rich landmark load. The barns hold grain, cloth, a little scrap and fuel. The hangars, the blockhouse, the guard huts and four army crate stacks hold army stores: parts, meds and scrap, with the best chance at a weapon or armor. The army trucks hold a road-wreck load. A truck parks beside a spot and searches it, and an emptied spot refills over about two weeks like the Fallen Sun's.
- **Groves.** About 500 dead trees stand in 15 blocks on both sides of the road and in the north grounds, on darker field ground, each block in rows along or across the road and set a little off its line. Single trees stand between the blocks, so the groves fray at their edges. Rows are 12 m apart and trees 6 m apart in a row, with a few missing. Each tree's trunk is narrow and its wide crown starts above a truck's roof, so a truck drives the lanes between rows. Rows block sight across them but not along the lanes, which gives ambush cover. Broken wooden fences edge the outer blocks.
- **Breakable trees.** A tree holds like a wall until a truck hits it at 3 m/s or more, which breaks it, scrapes the truck and slows it. That opens the row and the line of sight. Broken trees grow back after 3 days, only out of the player's gray vision. Gunfire breaks nothing.
- **Canals and dirt roads.** Blue-grey concrete irrigation canals run along both sides of the highway, broken where roads cross, and along the block edges. A truck in one drags and wears like dirty water. Dirt roads and tracks are packed pale dirt, as fast as hardpan.
- **Clutter and debris.** Fuel drums lie by the trucks and hangars, lumber and junk by the barns and the farmhouse, and loose sandbags and barriers by the guard huts, all scattered, never in rows. Junk piles and old car wrecks lie along the road.
- **NPCs.** Scavengers search the spots, and raiders and vultures hunt there.

Each town gate has a guard gun. Each turn it shoots the nearest vehicle in its range that fired at anyone but a raider, whatever its own faction. Raiders also trade in towns, so guards judge by action. A town gate is therefore a safe place to run to.

Raiders have two camps, Scrapjaw Camp in the north and Kiln Camp in the south. A dirt track leads to each camp gate. Raiders spawn outside a camp gate and patrol the roads around their own camp between raids. Each camp gate gun shoots the nearest non-raider in its range every turn, whether or not that vehicle fired. The player cannot use camp services.

## Sun, time and weather

A day is a fixed number of turns, and the clock starts in the morning. The sun rises in the east, crosses north at noon and sets in the west. There is no sun at night. A point is in shade when a hill, rock, wreck or building blocks the ray toward the sun. Heat is at its base rate in shade and at night, and rises toward its peak in full sun. Heat multiplies fuel and supply drain, so the wrong time and route cost more fuel and supplies. The ground darkens over explored shaded tiles, by the same rule the sim uses. The sun's light follows the clock, and the scene dims after dark.

Weather events come from the world's own randomness, so a seed replays the same weather. Dust storms are huge moving areas, and several can roam the region at once. Inside one, sight and aim both suffer, top speed drops, and wear climbs faster. The effects fade in over the outer 25 tiles of a storm, so riding in is gradual. A heat wave covers the whole region and strengthens the sun's heat. Overcast also covers the region, and cancels the sun's heat. The HUD shows the day, time, current heat and the region's weather.

The sun also heats the player's running engine, faster at higher speed. Airflow, shade, night and parking cool it, and parking in shade cools it fastest. Full noon sun at top speed overheats a cold stock engine in about 25 turns, while morning and evening sun barely warm it. An overheated engine loses HP every turn it keeps driving. Every engine starts heating at the same sun heat, where the ground shimmers, and engines differ only in how fast they heat. A racing V6 or a turbine overheats fast in the noon sun, and a diesel runs cool. Still, every engine overheats on the shortest Bowl to Nose trip at top speed, leaving at 10:00. The HUD shows the engine temperature as a gauge, and the log warns once when it runs hot. NPCs have no engine heat, since they have no rule for stopping to cool down.

Engine overdrive, on the O key, gives the player 33% more top speed and acceleration. It heats a driving engine in any sun. A cold engine overheats in about 16 turns at night and 8 in full noon sun. The G key pours 1 supply of water over the engine and takes away half of the full heat at once, with a cloud of steam.
