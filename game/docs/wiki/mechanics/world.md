# World

The map, sites, town and camp patrols, time of day, weather and engine heat. The principles behind them are in [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md).

## Map and sites

The map is a grid of tiles with a height on every tile corner, so the ground is smooth hills and valleys. Rain cut gullies and dry washes into it, slopes slid into scree, dry lakes left salt crust and mud, and wind piled deep sand into dune ridges up to about 3 m tall. Boulders lie at cliff feet and on ridges. Ruins of the old world stand on flat ground near today's places, on hill edges and on road bends: houses, silos, water towers, gas stations, dead fields, power lines, billboards and tank hulks. Faded asphalt roads join the old settlements. A few old highways ended in broken bridges over deep gorges. At Broken Wing a crashed ship's wing lies on lattice and pylons beside a crash trench. The road runs under the wing's bent root, a tall hoop, then up a sand ramp and along the wing's top, and trucks drive down at the far end. Ruins and structures block driving and sight, so they give cover. Squatters live in shack camps outside towns, among ruins and at junctions, behind wooden fences and junk piles. Burnt cars line the roads. Scrub grows where water gathers, and small basins hold dirty water or toxic pools that slow trucks. Everything collides by its real shape: trucks see over low fences, junk and wrecks, but not over walls. Each tile has a terrain type: road, hardpan, loose sand, scrub, scree, mud, gravel, salt crust, cracked asphalt, ash, dirt track, irrigation canal, cracked concrete or fused glass. Each type has its own driving speed. Fused glass is very slippery. Tire grip along the wheel there is a fifth of normal, so every truck takes longer to speed up and stop. Grip across the wheel is far lower, so a turning truck skids sideways through the turn. Drivers brake early on glass, so they still stop on a point, but they corner as on any ground and skid. Every other type keeps full grip, and loose ground still cuts grip through its speed. Uphill slows a truck, downhill speeds it up a little. Tiles too steep to climb are cliffs, and routes go around them. Hills and obstacles block sight, and the fog of war shows only what the truck sees. A trail of crashed-ship debris (hull plates, wing shards, escape pods, habitat cylinders and power cells) runs from Podfield past Broken Wing to the Fallen Sun's north-east rim, with lone pods and plates across the basin. It is cover and scenery only: it blocks driving and sight like other props, and holds no loot.

Danger is set by region, not by player level.

Faction squads roam the map. Places are discovered by exploring. Towns and locations are static places that trucks never enter, so they block driving. Small locations have one gate, where the first road crosses their edge. Towns and large locations have a gate on every road. Each gate carries a lamp on each side. The gatehouses of towns and raider camps carry the gate gun. A dust pad lies outside each gate. A site is used only from a pad. The ten inhabited sites (both towns, the oases, the pump station, the granary, the salvage yard, the lock and the raider camps) are fortresses: a closed curtain of walls at least three times as tall as a truck, with towers and a shut gate at each road. Their walls block driving and sight by their real shape, and trucks stay outside and use the pads. Five fortresses have their own look:

- **Bowl** is a terraced crater farm. It is a star fort: six arrowhead bastions of multicolored salvage panels, each with a timber watch hut on a concrete pillar at its point and at each shoulder, so the towers cover every wall from the side. Both gates stand in the curtains between bastions. Inside, the ground drops in crop terraces with fruit trees and stairs to a floor of houses around a pond, with a turning windmill, two water tanks on stilts and a greenhouse.
- **Dustwell** is a small square of rust corrugated walls between pale concrete corner blocks. A rocking pumpjack pumps water inside, beside tall tanks and a lit shed.
- **Granary** is a smooth pale ring with no towers and a recessed gate. Inside stand four silos, an elevator with a conveyor that carries sacks down to grain heaps, two open shelters and grain bins.
- **Salvage Yard** is a square of dark rusted plates with buttressed concrete corner towers. A slewing crane hoists a car over stacked wrecks.
- **Nose** is built around the wreck of an old colony ship that came down on a faceted rock mountain. Its ring of colored ship-metal panels and close-set banded towers with lit windows closes the yard in front and ends against the mountain's flanks. The mountain fills the back right and runs well past the ring, its foot on a slope of scree. The ship's patched, rust-streaked nose lies pitched up on a rubble mound with a row of lit windows, and its torn, skeletal rear runs into the mountain, with a radar dish turning on the nose. The open yard runs from the south gate round to the WNW gate, built up with scrap shelters with shut doors and lamps, a two-level platform with stairs up the rubble, a red tin awning over a stall, a water tower, a jib crane and a van.

At these five the gate stands flush in the wall, with open ground between it and the pad. The other five fortresses (Green Pit, Pump Station, South Lock and the two raider camps) are castles of masonry or scrap whose gatehouse stands out on the site edge. Gate lamps and lit windows glow at night. The abandoned sites keep wreck walls or fences. A fortress is discovered by seeing the ground near its pads. The truck must be stopped to use a town or search salvage. In reach but still moving, the action shows dimmed.

The Fallen Sun is a territory: a crater with no edge, gates or pads, so trucks drive in. It is a place to prowl, hide and fight over loot on the ruins of a ship.

- **The wreck.** One broken colony ship lies across the crater, laid out from the committee's level concept. An open ribcage tube runs north from the south floor to a squat hub with a ring on top at the centre. A broken spine leads from the hub to the bow, the largest piece, which lies with its closed nose against the north rim. Around them lie two half-buried arch shells, three ring-banded drums, shard clusters, a tilted tower slab, a fallen gantry and a few huts.
- **Inside the hull.** The ribcage tube and the arch shells are open at both ends and high overhead, so a truck drives in and through. Their walls block driving and sight, so a truck inside is hidden from the sides and seen only along the tube. No hull piece is a deck a truck climbs onto.
- **Basin.** The crater is an irregular basin, not a circle. Grey crag walls close the north and north-east as real cliffs that block driving and sight, with one drivable gap between them where a dirt road climbs out north. A red-brown scree slope runs down the west bank, a broad hill rises on the east rim, and the south lies open. Low swells roll across the floor. The floor has the wasteland's own sand, so nothing marks where the territory starts.
- **Dirt roads.** A web of grey-brown dirt roads, 10 to 12 m wide, loops round every large piece, so each stands on its own island of pale sand with two or more ways round it. An outer ring joins the three road ends, and spurs fray out past the edge into the wasteland and lead nowhere. Dirt roads are track ground: faster than loose sand, so routes follow them.
- **Debris field.** Plates, junk and dead trucks lie scattered on the islands, thicker in the south and sparse to the north, and pale plate fragments lie on the scree. Debris never lands on a dirt road. It blocks driving and sight like any prop, and gives cover and ambush lines.
- **Crash furrow and wing.** Past the open south rim a furrow runs about 180 m south-south-west, where the ship came in. A huge torn wing lies along it, 32 m wide. A truck drives up a ramp onto it from the ground, about 6 m up, crosses 88 m of span over two large hull drums that stick out on both sides, and comes down the far end. The wing's sides and anything under it are out of reach.
- **Flaps.** Four wing flaps stand propped up as jump ramps, two in the furrow and two on dirt road stretches of the crater floor. Each is 20 m long and about 1.4 m high at its lip, with clear ground ahead. A truck that drives off a lip at road speed flies and lands upright. The landing costs its wheels some wear, less than a breakdown. Routes never take a lip, so a jump is a move the player makes in manual driving.
- **Loot spots.** There are 24, each near a dirt road. Nine are caches inside or beside the hull pieces, two of them in the ribcage tube and one in each shell, and hold the old site loot. Sight inside the hull is short and an ambush can wait at an open end. The other 15 are field spots in the debris, four of them in the furrow beside the wing and the flaps, with a scrap-heavy road-wreck load. A truck parks beside a spot and searches it like a road wreck. Its loot lies hidden until searches turn it up, see [Economy](economy.md). A spot never leaves, and an emptied one refills over about two weeks, so some part of the territory always holds something.
- **Roads.** Three dead-end roads come down the basin bank from the west, the east and the south-east and end just inside its edge, where they meet the dirt road ring. The south-east road leaves the end of the Kiln Camp track, so raiders have a short way in. No road crosses the crater, so traffic does not cut through it.
- **NPCs.** Scavengers search the spots, inside the hull too. Raiders and vultures wait at the road ends and in the debris fields.
- **Reactor.** The ship's core glows green-teal in a breach in the bow's flank. A truck within about 32 m of it costs its driver health each turn, down to a floor, so it never kills by itself. The player may drive in by hand. NPC routes keep out of the zone.

Old Orchard is a territory too: a ruined farm under military occupation, in a wide basin west of the north trunk road. Like the Fallen Sun it has no edge, gates or pads, so trucks drive in. It has no reactor or hazard. Its edge is not a circle: it follows the basin's ridges and roads, about 430 m from south to north and 330 m across, and takes in a north-west pocket and the flat ground north-east of the north ridge.

- **Roads and entries.** Five roads cross the farm and leave it in five directions. A straight old asphalt road runs up the basin a little east of north and leaves by the north edge. A short spur road from the north trunk road ends at its south end. Four broad dirt roads leave it too: the crossroad curves from the farmhouse yard over the highway, round the blockhouse and out east to the trunk road; the south-west road runs past the motor pool and the barn and out south; the depot road runs past the hangars and out east to the trunk road; and the north field road runs west through the north-west pocket. Narrow tracks join the groves and the depot. Trucks also drive in over open ground. The old highway cuts through the north ridge at a road's grade, so a stock truck drives into the north part, and the army's dozer track climbs from that cutting onto the ridge shelf.
- **Buildings.** They stand at their real size against an 8 m army truck. A ruined two-storey farmhouse, 26 m long, stands west of the road at the middle, and a sandbagged concrete blockhouse just east of it commands the crossroads. Three rusty Quonset hangars, each about 22 m long, stand side by side on packed dirt to the north-east, and a fourth stands at the north-east depot. To the south-west, a gabled barn and its shed stand past a concrete motor pool where three army trucks are parked in a row at uneven angles. A second barn stands in the north-west pocket. Guard huts stand where roads enter. A derelict jeep and an army truck stand on the highway's shoulders. The rest of the army trucks stand apart: one stuck at the end of the grove track, one broken down on the north pocket field track, one abandoned on the crossroad's bend by the blockhouse, one broken down on the depot road and one run off the south-west road.
- **Defenses.** About 30 sandbag positions guard the entries, the crossroads, the hangar yard, the depot, the motor pool, the north gap and the ridge shelf. Each is one to three curved sandbag walls side by side, bulging toward the road or open ground a threat would come along. Steel hedgehog tank traps stand out in front of them on that approach, never behind the cover and never on a road. A broken perimeter of concrete barriers runs inside the farm's open east and south sides and across the north gap: nearly half the pieces are gone and a fifth were rammed out of line, so it has several open breaches, and every road passes through it. Concrete barriers stand askew along the road shoulders, round the hangar aprons and the motor pool, and by the blockhouse.
- **Ground.** Each building stands on levelled ground, and the farm's barriers, sandbags, tank traps and clutter stand only where the ground is flat enough under them, so none floats or sinks.
- **Loot spots.** There are 28, and each is its own stock. At a building or crate stack the prompt reads Search, then Loot. At an army truck it reads Search the wreck, then Loot the wreck. The farmhouse holds the rich landmark load. The barns hold grain, cloth, a little scrap and fuel. The hangars, the blockhouse, the guard huts and four army crate stacks hold army stores: parts, meds and scrap, with the best chance at a weapon or armor. The army trucks hold a road-wreck load. A truck parks beside a spot and searches it, and an emptied spot refills over about two weeks like the Fallen Sun's. Spots stand apart in every territory, so a parked truck reaches at most two stocks and the player drives between them.
- **Groves.** About 500 dead trees stand in 15 blocks on both sides of the road and in the north grounds, on darker field ground, each block in rows along or across the road and set a little off its line. Single trees stand between the blocks, so the groves fray at their edges. Rows are 12 m apart and trees 6 m apart in a row, with a few missing. Each tree's trunk is narrow and its wide crown starts above a truck's roof, so a truck drives the lanes between rows. Rows block sight across them but not along the lanes, which gives ambush cover. Broken wooden fences edge the outer blocks.
- **Breakable trees.** A tree holds like a wall until a truck hits it at 3 m/s or more, which breaks it, scrapes the truck and slows it. That opens the row and the line of sight. Broken trees grow back after 3 days, only out of the player's gray vision. Gunfire breaks nothing.
- **Canals and dirt roads.** Blue-grey concrete irrigation canals run along both sides of the highway, broken where roads cross, and along the block edges. A truck in one drags and wears like dirty water. Dirt roads and tracks are packed pale dirt, as fast as hardpan.
- **Clutter and debris.** Fuel drums lie by the trucks and hangars, lumber and junk by the barns and the farmhouse, and barriers pulled aside by the guard huts, all scattered, never in rows. Junk piles and old car wrecks lie along the road.
- **NPCs.** Scavengers search the spots, and raiders and vultures hunt there.

Glass Flats is a large territory, 47 to 95 tiles from its centre to its edge, filling the land between the south road, the Salvage Yard road, Kiln Camp and Green Pit. A short straight spur leaves the south road at the old site's junction and ends inside it, and a second spur comes in from the Salvage Yard road, which skirts its east edge on the way to the south road's bend north to Canyon Bridge. A dirt track leaves its south-west edge into the scrub. It has no edge, gates, pads, site stock or hazard, so trucks drive in. Ochre sand is broken by flat fields of fused teal glass that cover more of the ground toward the edge, with teal glass spires standing in them. The glass is slippery to drive on. A hollow ribbed ship engine lies half buried at the centre beside a collapsed engine frame, and roofless New World compounds, watchtowers and broken scrap walls stand along broad dirt tracks. Its loot lies in separate spots: the engine's caches are the richest, the compounds hold town stores, and dead trucks lie in the outer patches. Each spot has its own stock and refills by the territory spot rule. Scavengers, roamers and vultures work its spots.

Old-world loot spots lie off today's roads. A few old places hold one spot each, and most hold none, so a ruin is worth a look but is often bare. A place is a farmstead with its silo or water tower, a hamlet of houses and ruins, a lone lookout building on a hill edge, or a group of tank hulks. A spot is one building or hulk at least 5 tiles past every road edge, outside territories and away from sites, with ground a truck can reach beside it. Gas stations and houses at road bends never hold one. The committed map has 6: 2 farmsteads, 1 hamlet, 1 lookout and 2 hulk groups, out of 41 places. Each type has its own load, about a territory spot's size. Farmsteads hold grain and cloth, hamlets scrap and cloth, lookouts scrap and meds, and hulks scrap. All of them hold a little fuel and supplies. A spot may hold one worn spare part, and about one in four of those is a good engine at most one wear step from new: a workhorse or heavy diesel at farmsteads and hulks, or a tuned V8 or racing V6 at hamlets and lookouts. At a building the prompt reads Search, then Loot. At hulks it reads Search the wreck, then Loot the wreck. A spot never leaves, and an emptied one refills over about two weeks like a territory spot. Scavengers head off the road for one now and then.

No town or camp has a gate gun or a guard tower. Lawmen live in Bowl and Nose, spawn at their gates and patrol the roads around them. Within 12 tiles of a lawman town's gate, drivers rarely start a robbery or a fight, but a fight that reaches a gate goes on there.

Raiders have two camps, Scrapjaw Camp in the north and Kiln Camp in the south. A dirt track leads to each camp gate. Raiders spawn outside a camp gate. They watch the roads from off-road posts around their camp, on raids and between them. The player cannot use camp services.

## Sun, time and weather

A day is a fixed number of turns, and the clock starts in the morning. The sun rises in the east, crosses north at noon and sets in the west. There is no sun at night. A point is in shade when a hill, rock, wreck or building blocks the ray toward the sun. Heat is at its base rate in shade and at night, and rises toward its peak in full sun. Far from the player, shade is not computed and every point counts as open sun. Heat multiplies fuel and supply drain, so the wrong time and route cost more fuel and supplies. The ground darkens over explored shaded tiles, by the same rule the sim uses. The sun's light follows the clock, and the scene dims after dark.

Weather events come from the world's own randomness, so a seed replays the same weather. Dust storms are huge moving areas, and several can roam the region at once. Inside one, sight and aim both suffer, top speed drops, and wear climbs faster. The effects fade in over the outer 25 tiles of a storm, so riding in is gradual. A storm also builds over its first 30 turns and clears over its last 30. Its haze and every penalty follow that build-up, so a truck already inside feels it grow and ease off. A truck also takes about 10 turns to feel a storm in full after it drives in, and about 10 to shake it off after it drives out, even at the storm's full strength. Turning back at the edge only slows that change, so the penalties never snap on or off. The screen tint and the wind follow what the player's truck feels. A heat wave covers the whole region and strengthens the sun's heat. Overcast also covers the region, and cancels the sun's heat. The HUD shows the day, time, current heat and the region's weather.

The sun also heats the player's running engine, faster at higher speed. Airflow, shade, night and parking cool it, and parking in shade cools it fastest. Full noon sun at top speed overheats a cold stock engine in about 25 turns, while morning and evening sun barely warm it. An overheated engine loses HP every turn it keeps driving. Every engine starts heating at the same sun heat, where the ground shimmers, and engines differ only in how fast they heat. A racing V6 or a turbine overheats fast in the noon sun, and a diesel runs cool. Still, every engine overheats on the shortest Bowl to Nose trip at top speed, leaving at 10:00. The HUD shows the engine temperature as a gauge, and the log warns once when it runs hot. NPCs have no engine heat, since they have no rule for stopping to cool down.

Engine overdrive, on the O key, gives the player 33% more top speed and acceleration. It heats a driving engine in any sun. A cold engine overheats in about 16 turns at night and 8 in full noon sun. The G key pours 1 supply of water over the engine and takes away half of the full heat at once, with a cloud of steam. Overdrive reads the active engine, the first one mounted, which is the same engine that drives the truck. It cuts out by itself when that engine falls to 15% of its max HP or below, whatever wore it down, and the switch stays blocked until a repair lifts the engine above 15%. A repair never turns overdrive back on; the player does. While heat damages the engine, its sound turns strained and knocking, and it comes back to the healthy note over two turns once the damage stops.

The L key switches the player's headlights on and off at any time, even while a turn plays. NPC lamps come on at dusk and go off at dawn. The player's lamps light the way whenever they are on, dimmer in bright sun. The lamps change no sight or detection rule.
